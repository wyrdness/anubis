# Anubis - Egyptian God of Death and Mummification

## Overview
Anubis (Inpw in ancient Egyptian, also called Anpu) is one of the most iconic deities of ancient Egypt - the jackal-headed god who presided over mummification, guided souls through the underworld, and protected the dead. As the original Egyptian god of death before being partially supplanted by Osiris, Anubis remained central to funerary practices for over 3,000 years. His distinctive black jackal or dog form, associated with cemeteries and the color of fertile soil/rebirth, made him instantly recognizable and deeply revered throughout Egyptian civilization.

**Classification:** Mythological Creature, Death Deity, Psychopomp, Funerary God

**Key Characteristics:**
- Jackal or wild dog head on human body
- Black coloration symbolizing rebirth and fertile Nile soil
- Protector of graves and cemeteries
- Conductor of mummification rituals
- Guide of souls through the Duat (underworld)
- Guardian during the weighing of the heart
- Judge and protector rather than destroyer

**Cultural Origin:** Ancient Egypt (Pre-Dynastic period c. 3100 BCE through Roman period 395 CE)

**Danger Level:** Protective (dangerous only to grave robbers and evil spirits) 🟢🟡

## Historical Background

### Pre-Dynastic Origins (Before 3100 BCE)

Anubis emerged from Egypt's earliest religious consciousness, pre-dating written history and unified Egyptian kingship.

**Archaeological Evidence:**
- Cemetery locations at desert edges where jackals roamed
- Pre-Dynastic burial goods showing canine associations
- Jackals' natural grave-digging behavior observed
- Desert canid species' nocturnal cemetery presence
- Early amulets featuring jackal imagery

**Ecological Connection:**
The ancient Egyptians observed jackals and wild dogs:
- Scavenging at burial sites
- Digging for buried remains
- Nocturnal activity near necropolises
- Black coloration of some species
- Boundary between desert (death) and cultivation (life)

Rather than viewing this as desecration, Egyptians ingeniously transformed the threat into protection - the jackal became guardian rather than destroyer.

### Dynastic Development

**Timeline:**

**Pre-Dynastic/Early Dynastic (3100-2686 BCE):**
- Anubis as primary funerary deity
- "First of the Westerners" (Khenty-Imentiu) title
- Cemetery god before Osiris
- Royal burial associations

**Old Kingdom (2686-2181 BCE):**
- Pyramid Texts feature Anubis prominently
- Royal mummification overseer
- Son of Ra in some traditions
- Protector of pharaohs' bodies

**Middle Kingdom (2055-1650 BCE):**
- Democratization of afterlife beliefs
- Anubis accessible to all classes
- Osiris begins to dominate afterlife theology
- Anubis as Osiris's assistant/son in mythology
- Coffin Texts expand role

**New Kingdom (1550-1077 BCE):**
- Peak of elaborate mummification
- Book of the Dead standardization
- Anubis in weighing of heart ceremony
- Continued popular devotion
- Artistic standardization

**Late Period (664-332 BCE):**
- Continued significance despite foreign rule
- Animal mummification cult centers
- Persian period maintenance
- Saqqara as major Anubis cult site

**Ptolemaic/Roman Period (332 BCE-395 CE):**
- Greek association with Hermes (Hermanubis)
- Roman adoption and adaptation
- Mystery religion incorporation
- Continued into early Christian era
- Gradual Christianization

### Mythological Evolution

**Original Status:**
- Supreme death deity
- "Lord of the Sacred Land" (desert necropolis)
- "He Who is Upon His Mountain" (overlooking cemetery)
- "Lord of the Hallowed Land"

**Integration with Osiris Myth:**

As Osiris's importance grew, mythology adapted:

**Parentage Variations:**
1. **Son of Ra** (solar theology)
2. **Son of Osiris and Nephthys** (most common New Kingdom version)
3. **Son of Set and Nephthys** (alternative tradition)
4. **Independent deity** (earlier traditions)

**Key Mythological Role:**

In the Osiris murder myth:
- Osiris killed and dismembered by Set
- Anubis helped Isis and Nephthys reassemble body
- Performed first mummification on Osiris
- Became prototype for all Egyptian embalming
- Established ritual procedures

## Physical Description

### Standard Iconography

**Classic Depiction:**

**Human Body:**
- Muscular male human torso
- Standing or kneeling position
- Often wearing only short kilt (shendyt)
- Sometimes full royal regalia
- Always dignified posture

**Jackal Head:**
- Black-furred canine head
- Long muzzle and alert ears
- Forward-facing, watchful eyes
- Realistic animal features
- No hybridization - distinct animal head on human body

**Coloration:**
- Jet black fur (primary symbolism)
- Sometimes blue-black (night sky connection)
- Gold jewelry and regalia
- White linen kilt
- Skin tones vary in art (red-brown, black)

**Attributes and Symbols:**

**Was Scepter:**
- Straight staff with forked bottom
- Represents power and dominion
- Held in one hand

**Ankh:**
- Symbol of life
- Often held or extended to deceased
- Life-giving power in death

**Flail:**
- Sometimes included with royal insignia
- Authority symbol

**Imiut Fetish:**
- Headless animal skin on pole
- Specific to Anubis
- Placed at tomb entrances
- Meaning debated (transformation symbol?)

### Artistic Variations

**Full Jackal Form:**
- Reclining jackal/dog position
- Atop tomb shrines or boxes
- Watchful pose overlooking protected area
- Particularly common in sculpture

**Standing/Seated Deity:**
- Throne seating like other gods
- Royal regalia
- Hierarchical scale (larger than humans)
- Temple wall depictions

**Active Ritual Scenes:**
- Performing mummification
- Guiding deceased
- Weighing heart ceremony
- Opening of Mouth ritual
- Receiving offerings

### Jackal vs. Dog Debate

**Species Identification:**

Scholars debate which canid Anubis represents:

**Golden Jackal (Canis aureus):**
- Native to Egypt
- Cemetery scavenger
- Traditional identification
- "Jackal" in Egyptian texts

**African Golden Wolf (Canis anthus):**
- Recently distinguished from jackals (2015)
- May be actual species depicted
- Similar behavior and appearance

**Wild Dog/Feral Dog:**
- Pariah dogs common in ancient Egypt
- Cemetery associations
- Easier to observe

**Composite/Idealized:**
- May not represent specific species
- Artistic idealization
- Symbolic rather than naturalistic
- Multiple canid characteristics combined

### Color Symbolism

**Black Significance:**

Not associated with decay, but with:

1. **Rebirth and Regeneration:**
   - Color of fertile Nile silt after flood
   - Life-giving soil
   - Agricultural renewal
   - Death as transformation, not ending

2. **Night and the Underworld:**
   - Nocturnal realm of the dead
   - Hidden, mysterious processes
   - Protection in darkness

3. **Permanence:**
   - Black things resist decay
   - Bitumen preservation
   - Eternal protection

4. **Ancient Associations:**
   - "Khem" (black land) vs. "Deshret" (red land/desert)
   - Life vs. death geography
   - Liminal boundary

## Roles and Functions

### Mummification Overseer

**"He Who is in the Place of Embalming" (Imy-ut):**

Anubis's primary earthly function:

**Ritual Procedures:**
1. **Purification:** Washing the corpse
2. **Evisceration:** Removing internal organs
3. **Desiccation:** Natron salt preservation (40 days)
4. **Wrapping:** Linen bandage application with amulets
5. **Final Sealing:** Completion of transformation

**Priestly Practice:**

**Embalmer-Priests:**
- Wore Anubis masks during mummification
- Became the god temporarily
- Performed sacred, secret rituals
- Special workshop (wabet) for purification
- Lengthy apprenticeships

**Magical Recitations:**
While working, priests recited spells:
> "Anubis lays his hands upon you,
> He who is upon his mountain,
> He who is in the place of embalming,
> To make your body flourish,
> To complete your transformation."

**Material and Spiritual:**
- Physical preservation for spiritual journey
- Each step had religious significance
- Organs preserved in canopic jars
- Heart left in body for judgment
- Amulets placed at specific locations

### Psychopomp - Guide of Souls

**Journey Through the Duat:**

After death, Anubis guided souls:

**Stage 1: Tomb Departure**
- Anubis receives deceased at tomb
- Helps navigate initial underworld gates
- Protection from demons and dangers

**Stage 2: Underworld Passage**
- Twelve gates/hours of night journey
- Monsters and guardians to pass
- Knowledge of spells required
- Anubis's protection crucial

**Stage 3: Hall of Judgment**
- Escorts soul to Hall of Ma'at
- Presents deceased to Osiris
- Conducts weighing of heart
- Announces verdict

**Stage 4: Final Destination**
- If justified: guides to Field of Reeds (paradise)
- Ensures safe arrival
- Continuing protection in afterlife

### The Weighing of the Heart

**Anubis's Central Role:**

In the most crucial moment of Egyptian afterlife:

**Conductor of Ceremony:**
- Removes heart from deceased
- Places on scales with precision
- Ensures accurate weighing
- Guards against tampering
- Observes balance carefully

**Technical Executor:**
- Skilled in metaphysical measurement
- Impartial despite protective nature
- Cannot be bribed or fooled
- Serves Ma'at (cosmic justice)
- Reports to Osiris

**Protector Despite Justice:**
- Wants deceased to succeed
- But won't compromise truth
- Protective throughout process
- Comforting presence
- Guides regardless of outcome

**Cooperation with Other Deities:**
- Thoth records result
- Ma'at provides feather standard
- Osiris pronounces judgment
- Ammit waits to devour heavy hearts
- All dependent on Anubis's accurate work

### Guardian of Cemeteries and Tombs

**"Lord of the Sacred Land":**

**Physical Protection:**
- Guards against grave robbers
- Detects desecration
- Punishes violators with death or curse
- Maintains tomb integrity
- Watches over mummies

**Spiritual Protection:**
- Wards off evil spirits
- Prevents demonic intrusion
- Keeps ghosts from escaping
- Maintains boundary between worlds
- Protects both dead and living

**Cult Practices:**

**Cemetery Rituals:**
- Offerings at cemetery chapels
- Prayers before entering necropolis
- Protective amulets worn
- Respect protocols observed
- Festivals honoring Anubis

**Tomb Inscriptions:**
> "Anubis, foremost of the Divine Booth,
> Lord of the Sacred Land,
> Who gives burial in the West,
> May he grant a good burial in the necropolis."

### Judge and Protector

**Dual Nature:**

Unlike many death gods, Anubis combined:

**Justice:**
- Impartial weighing
- Truth enforcement
- Cannot be deceived
- Serves cosmic order

**Protection:**
- Cares for the dead
- Helps righteous souls
- Shields from dangers
- Compassionate guide
- Continuing watchfulness

This made him beloved rather than feared.

## Cult Centers and Worship

### Major Cult Sites

**Cynopolis (Hardai/Kasa):**
- **Location:** Middle Egypt, 17th nome
- **Greek Name:** "Dog City"
- **Egyptian Name:** "Place of the Jackal God"
- **Features:** Main cult center, massive sacred cemeteries
- **Practice:** Dog/jackal mummification, pilgrimage site
- **Significance:** Primary worship center

**Saqqara (Memphis Necropolis):**
- **Location:** Near ancient Memphis
- **Features:** Anubis as necropolis guardian
- **Catacombs:** Millions of mummified dogs and jackals
- **Priesthood:** Large establishment
- **Era:** Particularly important Late Period onward

**Other Important Sites:**
- **Abydos:** Associated with Osiris, Anubis prominent
- **Thebes:** Necropolis guardian at Valley of Kings
- **Giza:** Pyramid necropolis protection
- **Lycopolis (Asyut):** Another major center

### Sacred Animals

**Dog/Jackal Mummification:**

**Scale of Practice:**
- Millions of canids mummified
- Votive offerings to Anubis
- Pilgrimage industry
- Breeding farms for sacred animals
- Elaborate burial catacombs

**Purpose:**
- Offerings to gain Anubis's favor
- Messages carried to afterlife
- Merit for donors
- Economic support for priesthoods
- Devotional practice

**Modern Discovery:**
- X-ray studies show diverse ages
- Some died naturally, others sacrificed
- Elaborate wrapping even for small animals
- Bronze statues often included
- Industrial scale in Late Period

## Mythology and Sacred Texts

### Birth and Family

**Mythological Parentage:**

**Most Common Version (New Kingdom):**
- **Mother:** Nephthys (goddess of death/mourning)
- **Father:** Osiris (god of afterlife)
- **Conception:** Nephthys disguised as Isis
- **Raised by:** Isis (his mother's sister)
- **Half-brother:** Horus

**Alternative Traditions:**
- Son of Ra (Old Kingdom solar theology)
- Son of Set and Nephthys
- Born of the cow goddess Hesat
- Independent deity without family

### Key Myths

**The First Mummification:**

After Set murdered and dismembered Osiris:
- Isis and Nephthys gathered body parts
- Anubis reassembled the body
- Performed first embalming ritual
- Wrapped Osiris in linen
- Enabled Osiris's resurrection
- Established template for all mummification

**Protecting Osiris's Tomb:**
- Anubis guarded Osiris's burial place
- Fought off Set's attempts at further desecration
- Maintained vigilance for eternity
- Model for all tomb protection

**The Weighing of Hearts:**
- Appointed by Osiris as ceremony conductor
- Given sacred scales
- Trusted to judge fairly
- Works with Ma'at and Thoth
- Ensures cosmic justice

### Pyramid Texts and Book of the Dead

**Pyramid Texts (Old Kingdom):**

Spell 437:
> "The doors of heaven are opened for you,
> The doors of the star-filled sky are thrown open for you,
> Anubis has counted your bones,
> He has gathered together your limbs."

Spell 670:
> "Anubis, foremost of the Divine Booth,
> Claims your body for embalming,
> That you may ascend to the sky."

**Book of the Dead (New Kingdom):**

Spell 151:
> "Homage to you, Anubis,
> Who makes protection around the Osiris,
> Grant that I may come forth,
> Under your protection."

Spell 17:
> "I am Anubis on the day of the centipede,
> I am the Bull who rules in Heliopolis."

**Coffin Texts (Middle Kingdom):**
- Extensive Anubis invocations
- Transformation spells invoking him
- Protection formulas
- Journey assistance requests

## Greco-Roman Syncretism

### Hermanubis

**Greek Integration:**

When Greeks ruled Egypt (332 BCE onward):

**Combination:**
- **Anubis + Hermes = Hermanubis**
- Hermes: Greek psychopomp and messenger god
- Shared functions: guide of dead, boundaries, transitions
- Visual fusion: Greek body, jackal head

**Characteristics:**
- Wore Greek clothing (chlamys, petasos)
- Carried kerykeion (Hermes's staff)
- Jackal head retained
- Bilingual inscriptions
- Bridge between cultures

**Cult:**
- Popular in Alexandria
- Mystery religion associations
- Continued into Roman period
- Appealed to Greek and Egyptian populations
- Philosophical interpretations

### Roman Period

**Continued Worship:**
- Roman Egypt maintained traditional cults
- Anubis temples throughout empire
- Isis cult included Anubis
- Spread beyond Egypt to Rome, Greece, wider Mediterranean

**Imperial Connections:**
- Some Roman legion standards featured Anubis
- Death cult associations
- Funerary practices influenced
- Religious tolerance period (mostly)

**Decline:**
- Rise of Christianity (4th century CE)
- Pagan temple closures
- Cultural transformation
- Knowledge preserved by priests until end

### Unique Aspects of Anubis

1. **Mummification Focus:** Unique extensive embalming ritual oversight
2. **Protective Justice:** Combines impartial judgment with genuine care
3. **Physical Transformation:** Oversees body's preservation and change
4. **3000+ Year Continuity:** Remarkably stable worship and iconography
5. **Beloved Rather Than Feared:** Death god who inspires trust and affection
6. **Ecological Observation:** Direct connection to observable animal behavior
7. **Technical Skill:** Expertise in complex preservation processes
